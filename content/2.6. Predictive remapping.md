> **Predictive remapping** is the process by which certain neurons in the brain begin to respond to a stimulus **before a saccadic eye movement**, even though the stimulus is **not yet in their receptive field**, but **will be after the saccade**.

In other words:

- The brain **predicts** where a stimulus will fall on the retina **after** the eyes move,
    
- And **shifts the neural representation in advance**.
    

This prevents perceptual discontinuities across rapid eye movements.

---

## 👁️ Why It’s Needed

We make **3–4 saccades per second**.

- During each saccade, the retinal image shifts dramatically.
    
- Yet we perceive the world as **stable and continuous**.
    
- Predictive remapping is part of the mechanism that **preserves spatial continuity**.
    

---

## 🧠 Where It Happens

- **LIP** (Lateral Intraparietal Area) — one of the first areas where it was discovered.
    
- **FEF** (Frontal Eye Fields)
    
- **SC** (Superior Colliculus)
    
- **Extrastriate cortex (V3, MT, MST, etc.)**
    

These areas are involved in:

- Eye movement planning
    
- Spatial attention
    
- Multimodal integration
    

---

## 🧬 How It Works (Conceptual Steps)

1. **Pre-saccadic planning** begins.
    
    - The motor system is preparing to move the eyes.
        
2. A neuron in LIP has a receptive field in, say, the upper right of the visual field.
    
3. The system **calculates where that receptive field will move** after the saccade.
    
4. Before the eye actually moves, the neuron starts to **respond to stimuli that are currently in the** _future_ **receptive field**.
    

> This is **not feedback from the retina**, but a **predictive shift** of the receptive field itself.

---

## 🌀 Relation to Reference Frames

Predictive remapping requires:

- Knowledge of **eye position** and **motor command**
    
- Ability to **project future sensory geometry**
    
- It reflects a **dynamic transformation of reference frames over time**.
    

The brain effectively does:

> “If I move my eyes this way, this object will land **there** in retinal space — prepare that representation now.”

---

## 🧠 Computational Hypotheses

Predictive remapping may rely on:

- **Efference copy / corollary discharge**: a copy of the motor command is sent to sensory areas to inform them of upcoming movement.
    
- **Gain fields** that allow neurons to adjust tuning dynamically.
    
- **Hierarchical prediction** in cortical circuits (aligning with predictive coding ideas).
    

---

## 📚 Key Findings in Research

- **Duhamel et al. (1992)** first described it in monkey LIP: neurons responded to stimuli that were **not currently** in their receptive field, but would be **after** the saccade.
    
- It supports **trans-saccadic integration**: the brain matches visual information before and after an eye movement.
