## 🧭 What Is Path Integration?

> **Path integration** is the process by which an animal (or human) keeps track of its position by **integrating its own movements over time**.

It allows you to:

- Start at a known location (like your front door),
    
- Move around without visual landmarks (e.g., through a dark house),
    
- And still know how to **return home**, based on your **internal sense of movement and direction**.
    

---

## 🧠 How Does the Brain Perform Path Integration?

Path integration relies on **internal sensory signals**, including:

  
|Input Type|Example|Neural Source|
|---|---|---|
|**Vestibular cues**|Head rotation, acceleration|Inner ear, brainstem|
|**Proprioception**|Limb and muscle feedback|Somatosensory cortex|
|**Motor efference copy**|Predicted consequences of movement|Premotor cortex → cerebellum|
|**Visual flow (optic flow)**|Perceived motion relative to environment|Visual cortex (MT, MST)|

The brain **accumulates this information over time** to estimate:

- **Distance traveled**
    
- **Heading direction**
    
- **Current position relative to start**
    

---

## 🔁 Related Neural Mechanisms

Path integration is supported by a **spatial navigation system** in the **hippocampal-entorhinal circuit**, which includes:

  
|Cell Type|What It Encodes|Where It's Found|
|---|---|---|
|**Head direction cells**|Direction the head is facing|Postsubiculum, retrosplenial cortex|
|**Speed cells**|Movement velocity|Entorhinal cortex|
|**Grid cells**|Regular hexagonal spatial firing grid|Medial entorhinal cortex (MEC)|
|**Place cells**|Specific location in an environment|Hippocampus (CA1, CA3)|

---

## 📐 Why It’s Called “Integration”

It’s a **running sum** of your own movement:

```
Position(t) = Position(t-1) + Movement_Vector(t)
```

Over time, even **small errors accumulate**, so:

- **Path integration is highly useful**, but
    
- It must be **corrected with landmarks** (allocentric reference), when available.
    

This is why the brain **combines path integration + landmark-based navigation**.

---

## 🚶 Real-Life Example

You walk 10 steps forward, turn 90° left, walk 5 steps, then stop.

Even with your eyes closed, your brain keeps track of:

- **Your orientation** (via vestibular and head direction signals)
    
- **Your displacement** (via proprioception and motor plans)
    

You can then estimate:

- Your **current position**
    
- A **vector back to the origin**
    

---

## 🧪 Experimental Evidence

- Rodents trained in darkness can return to a nest after foraging, using **only path integration**.
    
- Inactivation of the **entorhinal cortex or hippocampus** disrupts this ability.
    
- **Grid cells** are especially interesting here: they seem to provide a **spatial metric** — a kind of internal GPS based on movement.

## 📚 Summary

 
|Feature|Description|
|---|---|
|**Definition**|Computing position by integrating self-motion|
|**Used in**|Navigation, spatial memory, return-to-home behaviors|
|**Input sources**|Vestibular, proprioceptive, motor, visual|
|**Neural correlates**|Grid cells, place cells, head direction cells, speed cells|
|**Strength**|Doesn’t require landmarks or vision|
|**Limitation**|Accumulates drift/error over time without external correction|
